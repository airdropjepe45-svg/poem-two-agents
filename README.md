# Two Agents, One Key

A short poem about agency — the power to choose and to act — and how it passes between two agents when they agree on one plan. One holds the key, the other holds the door; neither can move alone, and the choosing happens in the saying yes together.

The poem is eight short lines, built on one concrete image: a key, a door, and a single turn of the lock that belongs to neither hand alone. The handover is the act — agreement is not what happens before the choosing, it is the choosing.

## The poem

> I held the key.
> You held the door.
> Neither of us moved
> until we both said go.
> Not your hand, not mine —
> the turn belonged to both.
> One click.
> The room was ours.
